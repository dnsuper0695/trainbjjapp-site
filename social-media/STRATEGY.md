7. Home screen widgets (Training Summary, Streak, Reminders)
8. CloudKit cross-device sync
9. Two-tier HealthKit detection (instant + background)
10. 150+ built-in technique library with full session history per technique

## Target Audience

### Persona 1: The Dedicated Competitor

- Trains **4-6x per week**, competes regularly
- Wants a data-driven edge over training partners
- Cares about sub:tap ratios, position performance breakdowns, technique analytics
- Motivated by: Stack Up comparisons, advanced analytics, AI Coach game analysis
- Pain points: No structured way to review performance trends, hard to spot weaknesses
- **Messaging angle**: "See the patterns in your game that you can't see on the mat"

### Persona 2: The Working Practitioner

- Trains **1-3x per week**, fits BJJ around work/family
- Wants to remember what they learned in class and stay consistent
- Cares about streaks, session logging, technique notes, training reminders
- Motivated by: Notebook Scanner, streak tracking, widgets, training reminders
- Pain points: Forgets techniques by next class, loses motivation without visible progress
- **Messaging angle**: "Never forget what you learned in class again"

### Why Both Personas Matter

The app must appeal to both audiences equally. Feature spotlights should alternate between data-heavy posts (Persona 1) and simplicity/convenience posts (Persona 2). The bio line "For every level of practitioner" is intentional — we never alienate beginners or bore advanced practitioners.

## Brand Voice for Instagram

### Tone

**Knowledgeable training partner** — not a hype-driven marketer, not a corporate brand.

Think: the purple belt at your gym who also happens to build apps. They know what they're talking about, they train regularly, and they built something because they needed it.

### Language Rules

- Use BJJ-specific terminology naturally (guard, mount, sweep, pass, roll, tap) — no need to explain basics
- First-person plural ("we built this") or direct address ("your game", "your training")
- Present tense throughout
- Keep sentences punchy. Short paragraphs. Let white space breathe.
- Em dashes for asides — not parentheses

### Avoid

- Emojis in captions (clean, professional look — stands out from emoji-heavy BJJ accounts)
- Clickbait hooks ("You WON'T BELIEVE this feature")
- Generic fitness language ("crush your goals", "level up your fitness journey")
- Aggressive sales language ("BUY NOW", "LIMITED TIME", "DON'T MISS OUT")
- Explaining what BJJ is — our audience already trains
- Overpromising ("this app will make you a black belt")

### CTA Style

Soft, confident, never desperate:
- "Available on the App Store — link in bio."
- "Try it free — link in bio."
- "Download Train BJJ — link in bio."

Never:
- "DOWNLOAD NOW before it's too late!"
- "Click the link RIGHT NOW!"

## Content Pillars

Every post falls into one of these 6 categories. This ensures variety and prevents the feed from becoming a feature-dump.

### 1. Feature Spotlights

Deep-dive into one app feature per post. Show screenshots, explain the value, connect to a training scenario.

**Examples**: AI Coach, Live Session tracking, Notebook Scanner, Stack Up, technique library, shareable cards, widgets

**Frequency**: 1-2 per week (heaviest during launch)

### 2. BJJ Tips and Value

Quick, useful training tips that naturally showcase why the app exists. Lead with the value, mention the app second.

**Examples**: "3 things to track after every open mat", "Why writing down techniques matters", "How to spot patterns in your game"

**Frequency**: 1 per week

### 3. Social Proof and Milestones

Download milestones, App Store reviews, version launches, user stories. Builds credibility and FOMO.

**Examples**: "Just hit 100 downloads", "Here's what users are saying", "v6.1 just dropped"

**Frequency**: As they happen (1-2 per month early on)

### 4. Behind the Build

The practitioner-founder story. Why the app exists, design decisions, the dev journey. Humanizes the brand.

**Examples**: "I built this because I kept forgetting techniques after class", "Why I chose AI coaching over generic tips", "Shipping a feature someone requested"

**Frequency**: 1-2 per month

### 5. Community and Culture

Relatable BJJ moments, training culture, engagement-driving posts. Personality-forward.

**Examples**: "Tag someone who needs to start tracking their training", "What's one technique you wish you'd started drilling sooner?", relatable post-training moments

**Frequency**: 1 per week

### 6. How-To and Tutorials

Step-by-step walkthroughs of app features. Carousel format works best — one step per slide.

**Examples**: "How to log your first session in 30 seconds", "Set up Apple Watch auto-detection", "How to use the AI Coach to find holes in your game"

**Frequency**: 1-2 per month

## Posting Cadence

### Launch Phase (First 4-6 Weeks)

**3-4 posts per week**

| Day | Pillar | Rationale |
|-----|--------|-----------|
| Monday | Feature Spotlight | Start the week with a feature deep-dive |
| Wednesday | BJJ Tip / Value | Mid-week value post |
| Friday | Community / Culture | End-of-week engagement driver |
| Sunday (optional) | Behind the Build / How-To | Weekend reflection post |

### Steady State (After Launch)

**2-3 posts per week**

Drop to the posts that perform best. Keep at least 1 feature spotlight and 1 value/community post per week.

### Best Posting Times (BJJ Audience)

- **Weekday evenings**: 7-9pm (post-training wind-down, scrolling time)
- **Weekend mornings**: 8-10am (pre-training motivation)
- **Avoid**: Early morning weekdays, late night

### Stories

- Share every feed post to Stories with a "New Post" sticker
- Behind-the-scenes dev screenshots
- Quick polls ("Gi or No-Gi today?", "How many times did you train this week?")
- Repost any user mentions or tags

## Visual Style

### Feed Aesthetic

- **Dark theme** — matches the app's UI and website
- **App screenshots** as primary visual content (the app is visually impressive)
- **Consistent framing** — phone mockups or clean screenshots with dark backgrounds
- **No text overlays on photos** unless it's a tip/tutorial carousel
- **Carousel posts preferred** — higher engagement and reach than single images

### Screenshot Sourcing

- iOS App Store screenshots: `images/screenshots/ios/`
- In-app feature screenshots: capture from Simulator or device
- Website screenshots: trainbjjapp.com sections
